Snowblind

An atmospheric survival game where you're a musher who has to escape a mountain with your dogs before a huge storm rolls in and traps you. As technical artist, I built the deformable snow and the snowstorm, and both grow as the game goes on.

Part 1

Deformable snow

  • Landscape material
  • Nanite tessellation

The problem

Snow is everywhere in Snowblind, and a musher and a dog team crossing it should leave a trail behind them. A flat snow texture would break the illusion, and the snow also had to get deeper as the storm closed in.

What I built

A dynamic snow shader for the landscape material, using Nanite tessellation. It lets persistent prints be made, with real indentations in the geometry rather than just in the texture.

  • The snow also changes depth over time, getting deeper as the game goes on
  • [How the prints are recorded and kept, e.g. render targets or a runtime virtual texture]

Result

Everything that moves through the snow leaves tracks that stay, and the landscape itself shows time passing as the snow piles up.

Part 2

The snowstorm

  • Niagara
  • VFX

The problem

The storm is the clock the whole game runs against, so the player has to feel it getting closer. Filling an entire mountain with falling snow would be far too expensive.

What I built

A snowstorm VFX system made of three Niagara systems that I use around the player, creating the illusion of consistently falling snow. The storm grows in size and intensity over the course of the game.

  • [What each of the three systems does, e.g. near flakes, distant snowfall and blowing snow]
  • [What drives the growth: a timer, game progress or something else]

Result

Snow falls everywhere the player looks, for the cost of a few systems around one character, and the storm visibly builds until the end.
